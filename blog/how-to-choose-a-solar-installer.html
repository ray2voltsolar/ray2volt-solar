How to Choose a Solar Installer in India: 12 Questions Before You Sign

The cheapest solar quote is almost never the cheapest system over 25 years. Here are the 12 technical, regulatory, and contractual questions to ask, the balance-of-system details that cut-price installers cut corners on, and the red flags that protect your rooftop investment.

Key takeaways

  • A rooftop solar plant is a 25-year asset where balance-of-system (BoS) hardware causes over 80% of premature field failures.
  • Only empaneled vendors on the PM Surya Ghar portal using ALMM-compliant DCR panels can secure subsidies up to ?78,000.
  • Legitimate installers provide an itemized Bill of Materials specifying cable sizes, switchgear ratings, structure thickness, and earthing specifications.
  • Never pay 100% upfront; tie payments to milestone deliveries and retain 10% until DISCOM net-meter synchronization is active.

When you install rooftop solar on your home or commercial building, you are not buying a simple home appliance. You are commissioning a miniature power generation plant that must operate exposed to intense sun, monsoons, high-velocity winds, and thermal cycling for 25 to 30 years. Yet many consumers evaluate solar proposals on a single metric: the lump-sum price quoted per kilowatt.

That single metric conceals significant risks. In the Indian market, two contractors can quote for a 5 kW system with identical headline capacity, yet one quote is ?35,000 cheaper. That discount is almost always achieved by downgrading mounting structures to thin sheet metal, using domestic AC wires instead of UV-resistant solar DC cables, eliminating surge protection devices, or omitting chemical earthing pits.

When those shortcuts fail two years later, the cut-price contractor has vanished, leaving you with an underperforming system, recurring inverter faults, or a stalled DISCOM net-metering application. Use this 12-question checklist to thoroughly evaluate any solar EPC installer before signing an agreement.

Why Your Choice of Installer Matters More Than Panel Brand

Much of solar marketing emphasizes panel brands and cell technologies like TOPCon and Mono PERC. While quality modules are critical, they represent only half of a reliable power plant. Even a high-grade panel with a 30-year performance warranty will fail to deliver its expected 4 to 4.5 units per kW daily if strings are designed incorrectly, cables suffer high voltage drops, or modules are paired with an undersized inverter.

A solar installation requires coordinated structural, electrical, and regulatory expertise: civil engineering for 150 km/h wind resistance, DC electrical design for fire safety, and distribution utility liaison for net-metering approvals. Choosing the right installer is the single most important factor determining your 25-year energy yield.

Company Credentials and DISCOM Registration

1. Are you an empaneled vendor under PM Surya Ghar?

For residential subsidies under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana (?30,000 for 1 kW, ?60,000 for 2 kW, and up to ?78,000 for 3 kW and above), your installer must be registered on the national portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in). Unregistered electricians cannot upload commissioning reports or trigger the direct subsidy credit into your bank account. Always verify their national vendor ID before paying a deposit.

2. How long have you operated locally, and where is your registered office?

Digital aggregators often book orders online and subcontract labor to transient crews, leaving homeowners without local support when faults occur. Verify that the company has a physical registered office, local parts warehousing, and full-time technicians. An established regional footprint?such as Ray2Volt's registered office in Srikalahasti and operating points across Tirupati, Puttur, and Pichatur?ensures long-term accountability.

3. Who handles DISCOM net-metering approvals and clearances?

Physical panel mounting takes only two to three days; the real bottleneck is utility grid synchronization. Securing technical feasibility approval, arranging bi-directional net meters, coordinating DISCOM inspections, and obtaining electrical clearances requires dedicated regulatory coordination. A professional EPC manages this entire workflow turnkey.

System Design, Hardware Selection, and Bill of Materials

4. Which specific panel and inverter models are in the Bill of Materials?

Never accept vague proposals stating "5 kW Tier-1 Panels". Insist on a line-item Bill of Materials (BoM) stating the exact manufacturer, model number, rated wattage per module, cell architecture, and inverter efficiency rating. This prevents bait-and-switch tactics where high-end hardware is quoted but discontinued stock is delivered.

5. Are the modules DCR compliant and listed on MNRE's ALMM List-I?

To qualify for residential subsidies under PM Surya Ghar, MNRE mandates Domestic Content Requirement (DCR) modules where cells and modules are manufactured in India, listed on ALMM List-I. Following the June 2026 ALMM List-II compliance shift, even new commercial and industrial solar installations are predominantly engineered with DCR panels. Ask your installer for the module datasheet and flash test report with RFID barcodes.

Under PM Surya Ghar guidelines valid through 31 March 2027, central financial assistance is strictly capped at ?78,000 for residential capacities of 3 kW and higher. Non-DCR panels or unapproved imports are completely ineligible for this subsidy.

6. What structure engineering and wind rating do you use?

In South India, rooftops face extreme seasonal wind gusts during cyclones and summer squalls. Mounting structures must be engineered to withstand wind speeds of at least 150 km/h using hot-dip galvanized iron (HDGI) with an 80+ micron zinc coating or structural aluminium. Avoid pre-galvanized pipes with thin 15-micron coatings that rust within two seasons.

7. How are DC cabling, earthing pits, and surge protection designed?

Substandard DC hardware is the leading cause of rooftop electrical faults. Ensure your installer provides UV-resistant XLPO-insulated tinned copper solar DC cables, dedicated chemical earthing pits with copper-bonded electrodes (separate pits for DC array grounding, inverter AC grounding, and lightning protection), and genuine Type-II Surge Protection Devices (SPD) in both DC and AC distribution boxes.

Warranties, Service Commitments, and After-Sales Support

8. Who honours the equipment warranties and provides local service?

A solar installation carries three warranty layers: a 30-year panel performance warranty, a 7?10 year inverter warranty, and the installer's workmanship warranty. As detailed in our guide to decoding solar warranties, verify whether your installer handles warranty claims directly or expects you to courier heavy equipment to factory service centers.

9. What is your guaranteed response time during an inverter fault?

Every day your system remains offline represents lost savings. In summer, a 10 kW plant generates 40 to 45 units daily. Demand a documented Service Level Agreement (SLA) with a 24-to-48-hour response window and qualified local service technicians.

10. Is remote generation monitoring included and configured?

Modern inverters feature Wi-Fi or 4G monitoring modules that transmit real-time generation data to your smartphone. Ensure your installer sets up this monitoring portal during handover so you can track daily unit generation and detect faults immediately.

Pricing Transparency, Milestones, and Subsidy Processing

11. Is the quote turnkey or are there hidden net-metering and transport costs?

Low-cost proposals often omit crane charges, bi-directional net meter testing fees, and DISCOM application costs. Ensure your quotation is 100% turnkey and explicitly covers all statutory approvals, meter calibration, freight, and commissioning expenses.

12. What is the milestone payment schedule tied to installation stages?

Never agree to 100% advance payment. A fair payment structure aligns cash flow with physical progress and protects both parties.

Standard milestone structure: 20% on booking and design; 60% upon material delivery at your site; 10% after physical installation; and 10% retention upon successful DISCOM net-metering synchronization.

6 Warning Signs That Should Make You Walk Away

If you encounter any of the following warning signs during vendor discussions, pause immediately and seek alternative proposals:

Red flag warning: Never sign a contract with an installer who demands full upfront payment, refuses to specify cable brands in writing, or promises "zero electricity bills forever" without conducting a physical load audit.

  • Demanding 100% Payment Upfront: Legitimate contractors have credit lines to execute project milestones before final settlement.
  • Vague Quotations: Omitting exact module models and switchgear brands leaves room to substitute inferior components.
  • Unrealistic Generation Claims: A realistic generation rule of thumb is 4 to 4.5 units per kW per day annual average (1,400 to 1,600 units per kW annually). Claims above 6 units daily are misleading.
  • No Local References: Reputable installers readily provide references of operational systems in your district.
  • No Physical Office: Operating exclusively via mobile makes warranty enforcement nearly impossible.
  • Refusing Stamped Workmanship Warranties: Roof waterproofing, structural stability, and electrical workmanship must be guaranteed in writing by the installer.

Frequently asked questions

Can any local electrical contractor install a solar system eligible for PM Surya Ghar subsidy?

No. To qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy (up to ?78,000 for 3 kW+ residential systems), the installation must be executed by a registered vendor on the national portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in) using approved DCR modules. Unlisted contractors cannot submit commissioning reports or trigger the direct subsidy credit.

Why do solar quotes for the same capacity vary by ?20,000 to ?50,000 between installers?

Price variations reflect differences in Balance of System (BoS) hardware quality, structure gauge, and service commitments. Lower quotes frequently cut costs by using thin pre-galvanized structures, unbranded switchgear, generic AC wire instead of UV-resistant DC cables, or by omitting statutory net-metering fees.

What happens if my solar installer goes out of business after commissioning?

Major equipment warranties?such as the 30-year panel performance warranty and 7?10 year inverter warranty?are backed by manufacturers if you have original invoices. However, ongoing maintenance and warranty claims depend on the installer, making an established local company essential.

Should I pay the full contract amount before net-metering approval?

Never pay 100% upfront. An industry-standard schedule is milestone-based: 20% on booking, 60% upon material delivery at your site, 10% after physical installation, and the final 10% retention upon successful DISCOM net-meter synchronization.

How do I verify whether the solar panels delivered are genuine and ALMM compliant?

Every compliant module features an internal RFID barcode beneath the front glass containing its serial number, manufacture date, rated wattage, and cell origin. You can cross-check this barcode against the manufacturer test sheet and MNRE's ALMM List-I.

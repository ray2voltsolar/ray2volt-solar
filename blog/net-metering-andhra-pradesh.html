Net Metering in Andhra Pradesh: How Your Meter Runs Backwards

What net metering actually is, how export credits are settled with your DISCOM, the paperwork involved, and why the meter approval step decides your commissioning date.

Key takeaways

  • Net metering replaces your standard electricity meter with a bi-directional meter that independently records units imported from and exported to the DISCOM grid.
  • In Andhra Pradesh, your monthly electricity bill is calculated exclusively on Net Units (Import minus Export), drastically slashing higher tariff slab costs.
  • Surplus solar units are banked on a rolling basis across the financial year, with unadjusted units settled at the APERC-mandated rate on March 31st.
  • Net meter physical installation and joint DISCOM inspection are the mandatory milestones required before the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy can be disbursed.

When property owners talk about rooftop solar making their electricity meter "run backwards," they are describing the net metering mechanism. While modern digital meters do not physically spin backwards like mechanical disc meters of the past, their digital registers achieve the exact same financial result: every unit of solar power you export cancels out a unit of expensive utility electricity you consume.

For homes and businesses across Andhra Pradesh connected to APSPDCL, net metering is the regulatory engine that makes rooftop solar economically viable. It transforms your rooftop into a mini power station that feeds excess energy into the local distribution grid during sunny daytime hours and draws grid power seamlessly at night.

Whether you are implementing a residential on-grid system or designing a multi-kilowatt commercial solar installation, understanding how net metering functions under Andhra Pradesh State Electricity Regulatory Commission (APERC) rules is essential for managing your energy costs.

What is net metering and how does a bi-directional meter work?

A standard residential or commercial electricity meter is unidirectional — it only knows how to measure power moving in one direction: from the utility grid into your building. If you connect a solar plant to a unidirectional meter, any surplus power generated on your roof that flows back into the grid might either be ignored or, worse, recorded as additional consumption on your monthly bill.

A bi-directional net meter solves this problem with three distinct digital registers:

  • Import Register (kWh Import): Tracks the total units of electricity your building draws from the DISCOM grid during nighttime hours, cloudy intervals, or when appliance demand exceeds solar generation.
  • Export Register (kWh Export): Tracks the total surplus units of clean solar electricity your rooftop panels push into the DISCOM grid when generation exceeds your immediate household demand.
  • Net Register (Net kWh): Calculates the arithmetic difference between energy imported and energy exported: Net Consumption = Import Units − Export Units.

Instant Self-Consumption vs Grid Export: During bright sunshine hours, your appliances consume solar energy directly without the power ever touching the net meter. The net meter only measures the remaining surplus that leaves your premises, making solar self-consumption 100% efficient. Learn more in our guide on how rooftop solar works.

How solar export credits and energy banking work on your APSPDCL bill

In Andhra Pradesh, electricity distribution is managed across regions by state utilities including APSPDCL (covering Tirupati, Chittoor, Nellore, Kadapa, and Anantapur districts) and APEPDCL. Under APERC rooftop solar regulations, net metering operates on an annual energy banking cycle running from April 1st to March 31st. Households and businesses inside the APSPDCL belt can see how this process plays out locally on our rooftop solar in Tirupati page.

Each month, your DISCOM meter reader captures your Import and Export meter readings. Three billing scenarios typically occur:

Scenario A: Monthly Consumption Exceeds Solar Generation (Net Import)

If your home consumes 450 units in a hot summer month and your 3 kW solar system generates and exports 350 units, your net billed units equal 100 kWh (450 minus 350). You only pay energy charges for those 100 units at the lowest slab rate, plus standard fixed customer charges.

Scenario B: Solar Generation Exceeds Monthly Consumption (Net Export)

If your home consumes 250 units in December while your solar plant exports 350 units, your net consumption for that billing cycle is zero. You pay only the minimum fixed monthly connection charge. The surplus 100 units are not lost — they are credited as banked energy units on your electricity bill and carried forward to offset future high-usage summer months.

Scenario C: Annual Financial Settlement (March 31st)

At the end of the 12-month settlement period on March 31st, any net surplus banked units remaining in your account are settled financially by APSPDCL. The utility compensates these surplus units at the APERC-mandated average power purchase cost (typically ₹2.50 to ₹3.00 per unit), credited directly against upcoming energy bills.

Step-by-step APSPDCL net metering approval workflow

Securing a net meter in Andhra Pradesh follows a structured government-mandated procedure designed to ensure grid stability and electrical safety. The process is integrated with the national PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in).

  1. Registration & Consumer Verification: The homeowner registers on the national solar portal using their APSPDCL electricity service connection number (USC number) and Aadhaar-linked mobile number.
  2. Technical Feasibility Submission: Ray2Volt submits the formal technical feasibility application detailing system capacity (kW), proposed inverter make, and rooftop layout. APSPDCL evaluates the local distribution transformer (DTR) capacity.
  3. Feasibility Approval: APSPDCL issues technical feasibility clearance within 5 to 10 working days, reserving grid capacity on your local distribution line.
  4. Physical Rooftop Installation: Ray2Volt installs the solar array using ALMM/DCR-compliant monocrystalline modules and MNRE-approved inverters, complying with standard electrical safety standards.
  5. Work Completion & Net Meter Testing: The installer submits the Work Completion Report, inverter test certificates, and earthing test results. The bi-directional meter is procured and submitted to the APSPDCL Meter Relay Testing (MRT) division for calibration.
  6. Joint Site Inspection & Meter Installation: The APSPDCL Assistant Executive Engineer (AEE) inspects the rooftop plant, installs the tested bi-directional net meter, removes the old meter, and synchronizes the system with the grid.
  7. Commissioning Certificate & Subsidy Release: The DISCOM uploads the signed Joint Inspection Report (JIR) and Commissioning Certificate to the national portal, triggering the direct benefit transfer (DBT) subsidy into the consumer's bank account.

For a detailed breakdown of documents required at each milestone, read our step-by-step guide on how to apply for PM Surya Ghar.

Name Matching and Connected Load Compliance: Ensure the name on your electricity service connection exactly matches your Aadhaar card and bank passbook. Any mismatch between DISCOM records and bank account details is a leading cause of portal verification rejection and subsidy delay.

Technical standards, anti-islanding safety, and meter specifications

To obtain net meter clearance from APSPDCL, your rooftop installation must comply with strict Central Electricity Authority (CEA) technical regulations:

  • Anti-Islanding Protection (IEEE 1547 / IEC 62116): Grid-interactive inverters must automatically disconnect from the grid within 2 seconds if grid voltage or frequency collapses. This prevents the solar system from energizing utility lines during power cuts, protecting maintenance crews.
  • Dedicated Earthing & Lightning Protection: APERC regulations require separate dedicated earthing pits for DC surge protection, AC equipment grounding, and lightning arresters (LA) to safeguard household appliances from surges.
  • Dual Disconnect Switches: A lockable, external AC manual isolator switch must be installed near the net meter location, allowing DISCOM field staff to isolate the solar plant during line maintenance.

Three critical traps that delay net meter commissioning

Over years of managing DISCOM approvals in Tirupati and Srikalahasti, we observe that approval delays almost always stem from three common administrative oversights:

1. Installing Before Receiving DISCOM Feasibility Clearance

Some unauthorized installers rush physical installation before the DISCOM grants online technical feasibility. If the local transformer capacity is constrained, APSPDCL may reject the grid interconnection request, leaving the homeowner with an unusable solar plant.

2. Exceeding Connected Sanctioned Load

Under APSPDCL rules, your solar plant capacity cannot exceed 100% of your sanctioned load. If you require a 5 kW solar plant but hold a 3 kW single-phase domestic connection, you must file for a sanctioned load enhancement and three-phase conversion before the net meter can be inspected. Sizing your system accurately from the start is critical — refer to our guide on how to size a solar system.

3. Procuring Unapproved Meter Models

Net meters must comply with specific DLMS/COSEM communication protocols and be approved by the DISCOM testing lab. Ray2Volt procures pre-approved, utility-certified meters directly from authorized manufacturers to ensure immediate lab clearance.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a normal digital energy meter and a solar net meter?

A standard unidirectional energy meter only records electricity drawn from the grid (Import). If you export surplus solar energy back through an ordinary meter, it may fail to record it or erroneously charge you for that generation. A bi-directional net meter has separate registers measuring Import (units from the grid) and Export (units to the grid), enabling the DISCOM to bill only the net difference.

How long does APSPDCL take to approve and install a net meter in Andhra Pradesh?

Technical feasibility approval on the national solar portal usually takes 5 to 10 working days. After physical rooftop installation and work completion submission, APSPDCL field engineers inspect the site, test the meter at the departmental testing lab (MRT), and install the bi-directional meter within 10 to 15 days.

What happens to surplus banked solar units at the end of the financial year in Andhra Pradesh?

Surplus solar units exported during sunny months are banked and offset against consumption during low-generation or high-usage months within the same financial year. Any unadjusted surplus credits remaining on March 31st are settled by the DISCOM at the APERC-mandated average power purchase cost (typically ₹2.50 to ₹3.00 per unit).

Can I install a net-metered solar system on a single-phase domestic connection?

Yes, APSPDCL permits single-phase net-metered solar systems up to 3 kW capacity matching your single-phase connected load. For solar systems of 4 kW, 5 kW, or higher, APSPDCL requires consumers to upgrade their connection to a three-phase line before net meter commissioning.

Does the net meter continue exporting power during a grid power cut?

No. Standard on-grid solar inverters feature anti-islanding safety controls that shut down solar power export within milliseconds during a grid blackout. This safeguards utility maintenance technicians repairing distribution lines. For uninterrupted backup power during load-shedding, property owners should choose a hybrid solar system with battery storage.

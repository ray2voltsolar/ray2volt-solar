Rooftop Solar ROI: How Long Until Your System Actually Pays for Itself?

A payback estimate depends on the units your roof can produce and the grid charges those units replace. Use the home and business examples below to check a quote before relying on its savings figure.

Payback points at a glance

  • For homes, the typical payback after the PM Surya Ghar subsidy is 4 to 5 years, subject to a site audit and your electricity tariff.
  • For businesses, typical payback is 3 to 5 years. Daytime consumption and tariff matter more than the system size alone.
  • In this part of Andhra Pradesh, 1 kW may produce about 4 to 4.5 units a day on an annual average, or 1,400 to 1,600 units a year.
  • A useful estimate includes dust losses, panel degradation, cleaning and a future inverter replacement.

Your solar quote may show an attractive payback period. Before relying on it, check which units the system will generate, which grid charges those units will actually replace and what you will spend on upkeep. A low electricity bill after installation does not mean every charge on the bill disappears.

A rooftop system is a long-lived asset, so a small change in the generation or tariff assumption can shift the payback date. This matters for a Tirupati household paying a few thousand rupees a month and for a Sri City factory with a much larger daytime load.

We will work through the home and business calculations, then show what to question in a proposal. If you already have bills and roof details, you can request a commercial solar estimate. The load pattern of offices and IT parks also needs its own assessment.

What determines solar payback

You can start with one calculation. Divide the money you put into the system by the net bill saving it produces each year:

Simple payback in years = net capital investment ÷ net annual electricity bill savings

That answer is only as good as the inputs. Ask your installer to show these four items separately, with the assumptions beside each figure:

  1. Your net investment: Add the installed price of modules, inverter, galvanised mounting structure, protection boxes and metering work. For an eligible home, subtract the PM Surya Ghar assistance once received. A business should discuss any depreciation benefit separately with its chartered accountant.
  2. Expected output: Around here, a practical starting point is 4 to 4.5 units per kW per day averaged over the year. That is roughly 1,400 to 1,600 units per kW annually. Your roof's shade and orientation can change it.
  3. Value of each unit: This estimate uses residential grid tariffs of ₹5.50 to ₹9.00 per unit and commercial tariffs of ₹8.00 to ₹12.00. The relevant number is the charge your solar unit displaces on your own bill.
  4. Costs over time: Budget for washing, gradual output decline of about 0.5% to 0.7% a year and possible inverter replacement. Panels have a 12-year product warranty and a 30-year performance warranty. Inverter cover is 8 to 10 years, depending on the model.

A home payback calculation with PM Surya Ghar

Take a Tirupati district home using about 350 to 450 units each month and paying roughly ₹3,000 to ₹4,200. Those numbers are an illustration. The right system size depends on its bill history, usable roof and the local DISCOM's approval.

A 3 kW on-grid system would need about 240 to 300 sq ft of roof without shade, using the broad planning allowance of 80 to 100 sq ft per kW. A survey must confirm the space and roof condition.

Work through the numbers

  • Annual output: The example assumes around 4,200 to 4,500 units from the 3 kW system each year. Weather, shade and maintenance will affect the result.
  • Central assistance: PM Surya Ghar provides ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and up to ₹78,000 for 3 kW or more. Eligible systems need DCR modules and a registered vendor. The stated scheme period runs through 31 March 2027.
  • Bill reduction: At 4,350 units valued at an illustrative ₹7.50 each, the example suggests approximately ₹32,600 to ₹35,000 in annual savings. The amount you retain depends on consumption, billing and export settlement.
  • Recovery of your cost: After the ₹78,000 subsidy, typical home payback is 4 to 5 years. A site audit and final quotation are needed before applying that range to your house.

Look beyond year five: The panels carry a 30-year performance warranty, although that does not mean free electricity or zero maintenance for 30 years. One illustrative calculation puts 25-year avoided grid charges for a 3 kW home above ₹8,00,000. Treat that as a scenario to test against your tariff, output and future costs, not a promised saving.

How a business calculates its return

For a business, the useful question is how much daytime grid electricity the plant can replace. Tax treatment can help, but it should not hide a poor match between output and load.

For factories, cold stores, hospitals, hotels and retail malls, the example uses tariffs of ₹8.50 to ₹11.50 per unit. A facility running from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM may use 80% to 100% of its solar output on site. Meter data should establish whether that is realistic for your operation, including weekends and seasonal closures.

Items that change the business case

  • Depreciation: Section 33 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 provides a 40% written down value rate for eligible solar assets. Your chartered accountant should confirm your eligibility, tax regime and the actual benefit.
  • Module selection: Following the June 2026 ALMM List-II shift, Ray2Volt quotes DCR panels for new commercial and industrial projects unless an exemption has been verified. The approximate DCR premium is ₹9 to ₹12 per Wp, depending on the brand. Include it in the project cost before comparing options.
  • Payback: A typical business range is 3 to 5 years. The outcome depends on a site audit, electricity tariff, daytime demand and the tax position. It is not a guaranteed return.

Check the assumptions: Figures here are indicative and schemes can change. Confirm subsidy and depreciation eligibility for your case. Ask your chartered accountant about tax treatment; Ray2Volt does not give tax or investment advice.

Assumptions to challenge in a solar quote

A neat payback number can conceal optimistic inputs. Ask the installer to show the monthly generation profile and the billing rule used for exports. Then check these four common sources of error:

  1. High output claims: A promise of 5.5 or 6 units per kW every day ignores monsoon and low-output periods. For this region, start near the 4 to 4.5 unit annual daily average and adjust after a shade study.
  2. Dust: In dry months, soiling can cut output by roughly 5% to 15% if panels are left dirty. Plan regular cleaning with suitable water and a safe access route. Compare monitoring data before and after washing.
  3. Inverter life: The inverter warranty is 8 to 10 years, depending on the model. Include a possible repair or replacement around years 9 to 10 in a long-term cash-flow estimate.
  4. Export credit: Excess generation may be settled at an average power purchase cost of about ₹2.50 to ₹3.50 per unit rather than your retail tariff. Check the current DISCOM rule before sizing for exports. A system matched to your actual use can be more valuable.

Cleaning estimate: An illustrative model assigns ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a year to generation that twice-monthly rinsing might protect in dusty conditions. Treat this as a site-specific estimate. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning guidance and avoid washing hot panels.

Questions about solar payback

When does a home rooftop system usually pay back?

After the applicable PM Surya Ghar subsidy, typical home payback is 4 to 5 years. The range depends on your tariff, usable roof, output and site audit. Assistance for a 3 kW home system is capped at ₹78,000, subject to eligibility.

How does payback differ for a business?

A business often uses more solar power during working hours and may offset a higher grid tariff. Typical payback is 3 to 5 years after a site audit. Eligible owners may claim 40% depreciation under section 33 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, but should confirm the tax effect with their chartered accountant.

How much electricity can 1 kW produce in Andhra Pradesh?

Use roughly 4 to 4.5 units a day on an annual average, or 1,400 to 1,600 units a year, as a planning guide. A 3 kW system might therefore produce about 4,200 to 4,800 units annually before site-specific adjustments.

What does dust do to the payback estimate?

Dust left on modules through dry periods may reduce output by about 5% to 15%. Regular safe cleaning can recover some of that loss. Include cleaning time and cost in your estimate rather than assuming panels stay clean.

Which later costs should I include?

Allow for routine washing, inspections and a possible inverter repair or replacement around years 9 to 10.

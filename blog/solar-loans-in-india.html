Solar Loans in India: Financing a Rooftop System Without Big Upfront Cash

How solar loans work, typical tenures and rates, the EMI-versus-bill-savings test, and the documents banks ask for when the subsidy is part of the picture.

Key takeaways

  • Collateral-free residential solar loans are available from major public and private banks for up to ₹2–6 lakh under priority sector guidelines.
  • Interest rates for rooftop solar loans typically range from 7.00% to 9.50% per annum, with flexible repayment terms spanning 3 to 7 years.
  • In sunny regions like Andhra Pradesh, monthly electricity bill savings from net metering frequently cover or exceed the loan EMI.
  • The central PM Surya Ghar subsidy (up to ₹78,000 for 3 kW and above) can be credited directly into your account to prepay principal and cut loan duration.

When you decide to install rooftop solar on your home or commercial premises, the primary obstacle is rarely technical feasibility or roof space. For most property owners, the deciding factor is upfront capital. Even with substantial government incentives, a reliable 3 kW to 5 kW residential solar plant represents a meaningful cash commitment before any subsidy hits your account.

Solar loans have changed that equation across India. Instead of liquidating fixed deposits, pausing household investments, or dipping into emergency savings, you can finance up to 80% to 90% of your rooftop installation through structured clean energy credit. The monthly energy savings generated by your panels offset your loan instalments, turning solar into a self-funding home upgrade.

Whether you are planning a grid-tied residential setup under the national rooftop initiative or evaluating debt options for a commercial facility, understanding the loan structures, interest rates, eligibility criteria, and repayment maths is essential before signing your installation contract.

How Solar Loans Work in India

A solar loan is a specialised term loan designed to fund the purchase and installation of photovoltaic hardware, including modules, grid-tied inverters, mounting structures, and DISCOM bidirectional metering equipment. Unlike unsecured personal loans with double-digit rates, rooftop solar financing benefits from priority sector lending guidelines set by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

Because a rooftop solar installation generates measurable economic value by slashing recurring electricity bills, banks view solar hardware as a productive, low-risk asset. The equipment itself serves as hypothecated security, enabling public and private lenders to extend collateral-free loans for residential consumers up to standard installation limits.

The borrowing mechanism proceeds in structured disbursements. Once your technical proposal and site feasibility are approved by a registered solar engineering firm like Ray2Volt, the bank sanctions the loan. Funds are disbursed directly to the certified installer across key project milestones—typically delivery of materials, mounting completion, and final DISCOM net-meter commissioning.

Under the national PM Surya Ghar scheme, major public sector banks (such as SBI, Canara Bank, Union Bank, and Bank of Baroda) offer integrated digital loan processing through the Jan Samarth and National Solar portals, streamlining sanction turnaround times.

Residential vs Commercial Solar Loan Options and Interest Rates

Solar financing differs depending on whether the borrower is an individual homeowner or a registered corporate entity. Borrowing limits, paperwork requirements, and tenure terms reflect these operational models:

1. Residential Rooftop Solar Loans

For homeowners installing systems between 1 kW and 10 kW, public sector banks offer concessional rates starting between 7.00% and 8.50% per annum for applicants with CIBIL scores above 700. Private banks and NBFCs provide rapid digital approvals with interest rates between 8.50% and 10.50%.

2. Commercial & Industrial (C&I) Solar Debt

For factories, schools, and hospitals, financing options include equipment machinery debt and dedicated MSME green energy credit lines from institutions like SIDBI. Commercial solar loan tenures run from 5 to 10 years, with interest rates typically between 8.00% and 10.50%. The EMI vs Bill Savings Test: How the Maths Works

The core financial calculation in solar debt is the net monthly cash flow test: if your monthly electricity bill savings equal or exceed your monthly EMI, the solar installation is cash-flow positive from month one.

Consider a typical residential home in Andhra Pradesh installing a 3 kW on-grid solar system. In this region, a shade-free rooftop installation produces roughly 4 to 4.5 units of electricity per kW per day on an annualised basis, generating approximately 1,400 to 1,600 units per kW each year (about 360 to 400 units monthly for a 3 kW system).

If your pre-solar electricity bill averaged ₹3,200 per month, an engineered 3 kW on-grid solar system can eliminate 80% to 90% of that recurring expense through net metering, saving ₹2,600 to ₹3,000 every billing cycle. If you finance the net project cost on a 5-year loan at an 8% interest rate, your monthly EMI is roughly ₹2,400 to ₹2,800.

During the 5-year loan tenure, your monthly utility bill plus your loan EMI equals what you previously paid the electricity board. After year five, when the loan is fully repaid, the system continues generating clean power for the remainder of its 30-year panel warranty, delivering two decades of pure savings.

How Solar Loans Integrate with the PM Surya Ghar Subsidy

Under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana (implementation period running until 31 March 2027), central financial assistance is standardized across India:

  • 1 kW System: ₹30,000 central subsidy
  • 2 kW System: ₹60,000 central subsidy
  • 3 kW and above: ₹78,000 central subsidy (capped at ₹78,000)

Banks sanction the loan based on the gross project cost minus your required margin contribution (typically 10% to 20%). The government does not disburse the subsidy directly to the lender prior to execution. Instead, the central subsidy of up to ₹78,000 is credited via Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) directly into your linked bank account after the plant is commissioned with a bidirectional net meter.

When your ₹78,000 subsidy arrives in your bank account, you can make a part-prepayment on your solar loan principal. Most public sector lenders levy zero prepayment penalties on rooftop solar loans, allowing you to cut your remaining tenure nearly in half.

To ensure your installation qualifies for this central assistance, the system must use Domestic Content Requirement (DCR) certified modules and be executed by an empaneled vendor. Commercial and industrial customers are not eligible for this residential subsidy. You can review the complete subsidy workflow in our detailed PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide.

Eligibility Criteria and Document Checklist for Approval

Securing approval for a residential solar loan in India requires meeting basic credit and property ownership criteria:

Mandatory Eligibility Requirements:

  • Applicant Profile: Resident Indian citizen aged between 21 and 65 years (salaried, self-employed professional, or business owner).
  • Credit Score: A minimum CIBIL score of 680 to 700 is preferred for concessional interest rates.
  • Property Ownership: Clear ownership or co-ownership of the building where the solar plant is being erected.
  • DISCOM Connection: An active residential electricity connection in the applicant's name with zero outstanding dues.

Documentation Checklist:

  • KYC Documents: Copy of Aadhaar card and PAN card.
  • Address & Ownership Proof: Latest property tax receipt, registered sale deed, or municipal assessment copy.
  • Electricity Bills: Latest electricity bills (past 3 months) showing sanctioned load and consumer service number.
  • Income Verification: Salary slips for the last 3 months with Form 16 (for salaried) or 2 years of ITR with computation of income and 6 months bank statements (for self-employed).
  • Solar Project Documents: Technical quotation and feasibility estimate from an authorised solar EPC partner.

Step-by-Step Guide to Applying for a Solar Loan

To prevent delays in loan sanction and net-meter synchronization, follow this structured application path:

  1. Site Assessment: Request a comprehensive power audit from a registered solar developer like Ray2Volt to evaluate your shade-free roof space (roughly 80 to 100 sq ft per kW) and connected load.
  2. National Portal Registration: Register your consumer number on the PM Surya Ghar national portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in) and select your registered solar vendor.
  3. Loan Application: Apply for the loan through the integrated Jan Samarth portal or directly at your bank branch, uploading KYC, income proof, and solar quotation.
  4. Sanction and Agreement: The bank reviews credit eligibility and issues a sanction letter detailing approved loan amount, interest rate, and margin contribution.
  5. Installation: Ray2Volt installs the DCR solar modules, grid inverter, and earthing infrastructure, with the bank disbursing milestone payments to the EPC contractor.
  6. Net Metering & Commissioning: The local DISCOM inspects the plant, replaces the existing meter with a bidirectional net meter, and issues the commissioning certificate.
  7. Subsidy Credit: Upload the commissioning report to the national portal to receive the central subsidy via DBT, ready for loan principal reduction.

Key Pitfalls and Fine Print to Check Before Signing

Before committing to a solar loan agreement, evaluate these critical terms:

1. Prepayment Terms: Ensure the loan permits penalty-free part-prepayment so you can channel your government subsidy directly into principal reduction.

2. Interest Rate Type: Check whether your loan rate is floating (tied to repo rates) or fixed across the entire tenure.

3. Processing Fees: Verify documentation and stamp duty charges upfront. Many public sector banks waive processing fees during dedicated green financing drives.

4. Accurate System Sizing: Sizing your system incorrectly harms your financial return. An undersized system fails to cover your EMI, while an oversized system generates surplus power settled at lower off-peak export rates. Sizing based on your genuine consumption profile is key to maximizing rooftop solar ROI and payback.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to pledge property or collateral for a residential solar loan in India?

For most residential rooftop solar installations up to ₹2 lakh to ₹6 lakh under PM Surya Ghar partnerships, public sector banks and leading private lenders do not ask for property collateral. The solar equipment itself serves as hypothecated security along with your clean credit history and proof of regular income.

What is the typical interest rate and repayment tenure for rooftop solar financing?

Residential rooftop solar loans in India generally carry interest rates between 7.00% and 9.50% per annum, depending on the lending institution, your credit score, and whether the application is processed through the Jan Samarth or PM Surya Ghar portal. Standard loan tenures range from 3 to 7 years, allowing borrowers to match monthly EMIs closely with electricity bill savings.

How does the central government PM Surya Ghar subsidy interact with my solar loan?

Under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, the central financial assistance (₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW, and up to ₹78,000 for 3 kW and above) is credited directly to your bank account following net-meter commissioning and joint inspection. You can use this lump-sum credit to prepay part of your loan principal, which immediately lowers your remaining EMIs or shortens your loan tenure.

Can commercial and industrial businesses also access rooftop solar loans?

Yes. Commercial and industrial enterprises can access commercial solar loans through public banks, private institutions, and specialised lenders like SIDBI under green MSME schemes. Commercial solar loans feature tenures of 5 to 10 years and can be paired with the 40% accelerated depreciation tax benefit under Section 32 of the Income Tax Act.

Will my monthly power bill savings cover my solar loan EMI from month one?

On properly sized on-grid systems in Andhra Pradesh, where a 1 kW system generates an annual average of 4 to 4.5 units per day (roughly 1,400 to 1,600 units annually), the electricity bill reduction frequently equals or exceeds the monthly loan EMI over a 5 to 7 year repayment schedule. Once the loan is cleared, the system provides free electricity for the remainder of its 30-year performance life.

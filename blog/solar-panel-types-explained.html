Solar Panel Types Explained: Mono PERC, TOPCon, and Bifacial

Mono PERC, TOPCon and bifacial panels use roof space differently. Compare their output in heat, warranty curves and mounting needs before paying more.

Points to take from the comparison

  • Typical TOPCon efficiency is 22.0% to 22.8%, against 20.5% to 21.5% for Mono PERC. This can fit more capacity on a tight roof.
  • TOPCon has a typical power temperature coefficient of -0.30% per °C, against -0.35% per °C for Mono PERC. The difference can add roughly 3% to 5% to summer units.
  • A raised bifacial array over a pale surface may gain 5% to 15% from rear light. A flush sheet-roof array may gain 0%.
  • N-type TOPCon avoids boron-oxygen LID. Later annual degradation is around 0.4%, with a 30-year performance warranty.

Your solar quotations may specify 540 Wp Mono PERC, 580 Wp TOPCon or a glass-glass bifacial panel. They look much alike on the terrace. Their cells behave differently in the 42°C May heat of Andhra Pradesh. The choice affects capacity on your usable roof, output in hot weather and generation over 30 years.

A wattage label alone cannot settle the choice. Check your clear roof area, mounting height, shade pattern and the total price. Bifacial glass, for example, adds little on a sheet roof where light cannot reach its underside. Here are the cell design, heat losses, ageing figures and roof conditions to compare.

How solar cell designs changed

Blue, speckled polycrystalline panels served installations for over two decades. At about 15% to 17% efficiency, they needed more area for the same capacity. Improved manufacturing made single-crystal monocrystalline silicon a practical standard. Mono PERC and TOPCon are different ways of extracting more electricity from that base.

How Mono PERC uses rear reflection

PERC stands for Passivated Emitter and Rear Cell. A conventional P-type cell loses some light at its back surface. PERC adds a thin rear layer with small contact openings. It reflects otherwise unused light into the silicon for another chance to release electrons. That is how it improves output over an older plain monocrystalline cell.

Mono PERC was common on Indian home and business roofs from 2018 to 2023. Module efficiency generally reached 20.5% to 21.5%. P-type silicon contains boron; sunlight and oxygen can form boron-oxygen defects during initial use. This light-induced degradation, or LID, may remove 1.5% to 2.5% of nameplate power in the first year. Read the first-year allowance on the warranty curve.

Why TOPCon uses N-type silicon

TOPCon instead uses N-type silicon doped with phosphorus. Without boron, it avoids the boron-oxygen LID mechanism. Manufacturers adopted the wafer to go beyond P-type PERC efficiency. The difference matters over a long service life because small annual losses add up across many electricity bills.

Tunnel Oxide Passivated Contact adds an oxide layer about 1 to 2 nanometres thick and a doped polycrystalline silicon contact film. This reduces electron loss at the cell surface while letting current pass. Commercial modules reach roughly 22.0% to 22.8% efficiency, with higher open-circuit voltage and useful low-light output in cloudy mornings and monsoon weather.

Cell design: In N-type TOPCon silicon, freed electrons can travel further before recombining than in P-type silicon. That helps retain output at low irradiance. Still, compare the complete module datasheets; cell type alone cannot predict the yield of your installed array.

Efficiency, heat loss and ageing

For your quotation, compare module efficiency, the power temperature coefficient and the promised degradation curve. Together, these affect the capacity you can fit now and the units your roof may deliver years later. The values here are typical examples. The maker's figures for the exact model should govern a purchase decision.

Power from a fixed roof area

A 72-cell or 144-half-cut Mono PERC module commonly carries a 535 Wp to 550 Wp rating. A TOPCon module of similar size may offer 575 Wp to 590 Wp. That is around 6% to 8% more stated capacity in the same footprint. It helps when water tanks, setbacks and access paths restrict panel placement.

If your Tirupati terrace has 250 sq ft of clear area, the module layout may determine whether the system reaches 3 kW or stops near 2.4 kW. Ask for a drawing and annual unit estimate with your residential solar proposal. Fewer panels for a given capacity can cut rails, clamps and DC cable per watt, but compare the full installed cost.

Output when panels get hot

Factory ratings use Standard Test Conditions, or STC, with cells at 25°C. A dark module in direct sun runs much hotter than the air. On a sunny Rayalaseema or coastal Andhra Pradesh roof, cell temperature can reach 55°C to 68°C. The temperature on your weather app should not be used as the panel temperature.

As cells heat, voltage and power fall. The Pmax temperature coefficient states the percentage of rated power lost for every degree above 25°C. Read that number on the exact model's datasheet. Panels with the same cell label can still have different thermal performance.

  • Mono PERC: Typical Pmax loss is -0.34% to -0.37% per °C above 25°C.
  • TOPCon: Typical Pmax loss is -0.29% to -0.30% per °C above 25°C.

At a cell temperature of 60°C, the panel sits 35°C above the test point. At -0.35% per °C, Mono PERC loses about 12.25%, calculated as 35 × 0.35%. At -0.30% per °C, TOPCon loses about 10.5%. Across a summer with air temperatures over 40°C, that advantage can add roughly 3% to 5% in units. Ventilation and site weather also matter.

What the warranty curve means

All modules lose some output over time from UV exposure, heat cycles and physical stress. The first-year limit and later annual limit are separate promises in a performance warranty. Check both, together with claim conditions and exclusions. The two cell types typically follow different degradation curves.

  • First year: Mono PERC may lose 1.5% to 2.0%, including LID. TOPCon allows 1.0% or less.
  • Years 2 to 30: Typical yearly loss is about 0.55% for Mono PERC and 0.40% for TOPCon.

At year 30, an example TOPCon warranty curve may promise about 87.4% of original rated output. The comparable Mono PERC figure is about 84.8%. These are warranty thresholds, not exact forecasts for every panel. Pair the performance curve with the separate 12-year product warranty when evaluating the offer.

When bifacial panels produce extra power

A monofacial module collects light at the front and normally has an opaque polymer backsheet. A bifacial module exposes its rear cells through transparent film or tempered glass. It can use light reaching both sides. Glass-glass versions weigh more, so the roof and mounting structure must be checked for that load.

The underside receives light reflected by the roof, called albedo, and diffuse light scattered through the sky. The rear response relative to the front is the bifaciality factor. The example is around 70% for Mono PERC bifacial cells and 80% to 85% for TOPCon. It does not mean 80% more rooftop units. Actual rear gain depends on available reflected light.

Check rear light: A bifacial module installed flush against dark corrugated metal or a sheet roof has almost no light underneath. Rear gain can be 0%. Paying for heavier glass brings no matching generation benefit there. Ask the installer for a mounting section and a separate rear-gain estimate.

Conditions for useful rear gain

A 5% to 15% bifacial yield increase needs a suitable layout. Before accepting that estimate, look at height above the roof, surface colour, row spacing and any structure that shades the back. The proposal should state these assumptions clearly enough to inspect on site.

  1. Panel height: Put the lower edge at least 0.5 to 1 metre, or 1.5 to 3 ft, above the roof so reflected light can reach the back.
  2. Roof reflectivity: Old grey concrete or dark asphalt reflects about 15% to 20% of light. White cooling paint or pale mosaic can raise that to 60% to 75%.
  3. Row spacing and cables: Leave enough pitch to prevent one row shading the surface below the next. Keep cables and purlins clear of rear cells.

Dual glass: Glass on both faces can limit moisture ingress, micro-cracks and potential-induced degradation, or PID. That helps in humid coastal conditions. Check module weight and mounting compatibility before choosing it for durability.

DCR and ALMM requirements

Indian panel selection also has a compliance side. The Approved List of Models and Manufacturers, or ALMM, identifies approved products. Domestic Content Requirement, or DCR, concerns where cells and modules are made. Ask the supplier for the precise model and its compliance documents.

For PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, a qualifying grid-connected home system needs DCR modules and a registered vendor. Indicative central assistance is ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and up to ₹78,000 for 3 kW and above. These are capped amounts subject to eligibility. Read our DCR and ALMM guide for the product rules.

MNRE maintains ALMM List-I for modules and List-II for cells. Following the June 2026 List-II shift, Ray2Volt prepares new commercial and industrial quotes with DCR panels unless a project has a verified exemption. Indian manufacturers also produce N-type TOPCon cells. A compliant choice can therefore use newer cell technology. Confirm the bill of materials before ordering.

The indicative DCR premium is roughly ₹9 to ₹12 per Wp, or ₹9,000 to ₹12,000 per kWp, against a comparable non-DCR module. Brand and availability change the gap. For an eligible home, compare the premium with the subsidy and the total installation cost. Do not assume every higher-priced panel offers better value.

Match the panel to your roof

Start with the roof. Measure clear area, check morning and afternoon shade, confirm structural capacity and decide how the array will be mounted. Then compare annual unit estimates and installed prices. The following roof layouts explain why a single panel specification does not suit every building.

Flat terraces for 2 kW to 10 kW homes

On a flat terrace for a 2 kW to 10 kW home system, you may want to keep space for walking, clothes drying or sitting. A 7 to 9 ft raised structure can preserve it. TOPCon bifacial modules may add up to 10% output if a reflective roof lights their backs. On low frames with little clearance, monofacial TOPCon gives high front-side wattage without paying for unused rear gain.

Industrial sheet roofs at 20 kW to 500 kW+

Factory roofs with corrugated metal or sandwich PUF panels, such as those in our manufacturing solar guidance and commercial solar overview, often hold modules close to the slope on short aluminium rails. At 20 kW to 500 kW+ scale, rear light is scarce. Monofacial TOPCon brings useful heat performance and power density without relying on bifacial output. Check fixings, corrosion and structural capacity too.

Roofs with shade or several orientations

Tanks, parapets and neighbouring buildings can shade different roof sections at different hours. Choose electrical layout with the panel. TOPCon half-cut modules combined with separate MPPT inputs or microinverters can isolate an affected group from a clear one. Our inverter and string sizing guide explains the options. Ask for a shade map before accepting the yield forecast.

Whatever cell technology you choose, request the maker's certificate for the actual modules and a written workmanship commitment. Read the 12-year product and 30-year performance warranties separately. Our panel warranty guide explains the clauses. Keep serial numbers and commissioning records with your system papers.

Scheme and price figures: Subsidy amounts, ALMM rules and the DCR premium here are indicative and can change. Confirm current rules, your eligibility and the proposed product during a site audit. Ray2Volt does not give tax or investment advice.

Common questions about panel choice

How do Mono PERC and TOPCon panels differ?

Mono PERC uses a rear-passivated P-type cell and commonly reaches 20.5% to 21.5% module efficiency. N-type TOPCon adds a tunnel oxide contact and commonly reaches 22.0% to 22.8%. TOPCon's typical heat coefficient is about -0.30% per °C against -0.35% per °C, and it avoids boron-oxygen LID.

When does a bifacial panel justify its added cost?

It can, if a light or white terrace surface reflects onto panels raised at least 1.5 to 2 ft above it. Such a layout may add 5% to 15% annual yield. Ask for the height and reflectivity assumptions in the forecast. A low mounting over a dark roof produces much less rear gain.

What does Andhra Pradesh heat do to panel output?

Power falls as cell temperature rises above the 25°C test point. In the example 55°C to 65°C roof conditions, Mono PERC may lose roughly 10% to 14% of rated output. TOPCon may lose about 8% to 11%. The selected model's coefficient and ventilation determine your estimate.

Are DCR modules required for PM Surya Ghar?

Yes. Assistance of up to ₹78,000 for an eligible home system of 3 kW or more requires DCR panels, among other conditions. The modules must meet applicable ALMM rules and the installation must use a registered vendor. Confirm your own eligibility and current terms before ordering.

Which panel warranties and output limits should I check?

The panels quoted here carry a 12-year product and a 30-year performance warranty. Example year-30 thresholds are about 84.8% for Mono PERC and 87.4% for TOPCon, based on later annual losses near 0.55% and 0.4%. Read the exact maker's curve and exclusions.

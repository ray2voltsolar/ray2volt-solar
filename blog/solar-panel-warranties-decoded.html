Solar Warranties Decoded: Product, Performance, and Workmanship Cover

A 30-year number on a marketing brochure gives homeowners and business owners a false sense of security. Solar installations actually rely on three separate warranty layers — product, performance, and workmanship. Here is how to evaluate each one, who is legally responsible for claims, and how to protect your investment.

Key takeaways

  • A rooftop solar system does not have one unified warranty; it is protected by three separate tiers covering product integrity, power degradation, and installation workmanship.
  • The widely advertised 30-year panel performance warranty only guarantees electricity output rates over time, not physical hardware defects like cracked glass or burned junction boxes.
  • Solar inverters carry a 7 to 10 year manufacturer warranty, and lithium batteries carry a 10-year warranty, meaning key power electronics may require replacement during your system's life.
  • Improper maintenance, harsh chemical washing, uncertified electrical modifications, and missing serial tags will void manufacturer warranty claims across India.

When you evaluate a quotation for a rooftop solar system, the prominent headline almost always highlights a 25 or 30-year warranty. For many property owners in Andhra Pradesh, this single number creates the impression that the entire installation — from the glass panels on the terrace down to the inverter on the wall and the cables in between — is fully guaranteed against failure for three decades. In reality, no solar plant operates under a single, all-inclusive 30-year promise.

A solar installation is an engineered electrical generating station consisting of multiple distinct components manufactured by different companies and assembled on-site by a local EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) contractor. Each piece of hardware has its own operating parameters, design lifespan, and independent warranty document. Treating these disparate protections as a single warranty can lead to expensive surprises when a component fails five or seven years into operation.

Understanding what each warranty layer covers, who is legally obligated to service it, and which common real-world actions void your coverage is essential before signing any contract. Whether you are installing a residential system through residential rooftop solar or investing in a large commercial array via our commercial solar solutions, this guide decodes every warranty tier so you know exactly where you stand.

The Three Separate Warranty Layers in Every Solar System

To evaluate your true coverage, you must separate a solar quotation into three fundamental categories: equipment product warranties, panel performance warranties, and EPC workmanship warranties. Each addresses a completely different type of risk, runs for a different duration, and is underwritten by a different legal entity.

The equipment product warranty covers the physical integrity of the manufactured hardware against defects in materials, assembly, and factory workmanship. If a bypass diode shorts out, an internal solder joint fails, or an inverter screen goes black during the product warranty term, the manufacturer is obligated to repair or replace that specific item.

The performance warranty, in contrast, applies exclusively to the photovoltaic modules and guarantees that the silicon cells will not lose generating efficiency faster than an agreed degradation curve. It does not promise that the panel will remain physically intact or functional if external components fail.

Finally, the workmanship warranty is provided directly by your local installation company. It covers the quality of the on-site physical mounting, electrical connections, distribution boards, earthing pits, conduit routing, and weatherproofing on your roof. Even if you purchase top-tier Tier-1 panels, a poor installation that leads to roof leaks or loose DC wiring is entirely the responsibility of the installer, not the module manufacturer.

Product Warranty vs. Performance Warranty: What Is the Difference?

The distinction between a solar panel's product warranty and its performance warranty is the single most misunderstood concept in the rooftop solar industry. Sales representatives frequently cite the 30-year performance guarantee while glossing over the shorter 10 to 15-year product warranty.

Consider a practical example: seven years after installation, moisture penetrates the edge seal of a solar module, causing visible brown delamination across the front glass and causing the internal circuits to corrode into an open circuit. Because this failure stems from a physical manufacturing defect in the lamination materials, it is evaluated under the product warranty. If your module only came with a 10-year product warranty, you are covered. However, if the same failure occurs in year twelve under a 10-year product warranty, the manufacturer can legitimately reject the claim — even though the 30-year performance warranty is still active.

Performance warranties follow a linear degradation schedule. High-efficiency modern modules typically guarantee that power output will not degrade by more than 1% to 2% during the first year of operation, followed by an annual degradation rate of roughly 0.4% to 0.55% per year for the remaining 29 years. By year 30, the panel is guaranteed to produce no less than 80% to 87.4% of its original rated nameplate capacity.

Degradation claims require factory testing: To successfully claim under a 30-year performance warranty, manufacturers require the module to be tested under Standard Test Conditions (STC) in a certified laboratory. Homeowners cannot simply point to a lower monthly electricity bill as proof of degradation, because weather, dust, and grid availability also affect generation.

Inverter, Structure, and Battery Storage Warranties

While solar panels have no moving parts and can operate for several decades, the balance-of-system components experience continuous electrical and thermal stress. The inverter is the hardest-working component in your solar power system, continuously converting direct current (DC) power from the roof into 230V or 415V alternating current (AC) power synchronised with the DISCOM grid.

On-grid string inverters standardly carry a 7 to 10-year manufacturer warranty. Because power electronics capacitors and semiconductor switches degrade under high ambient temperatures, an inverter operating in the climate of Andhra Pradesh has an expected operating lifespan of 10 to 14 years. This means property owners should anticipate replacing or refurbishing their string inverter at least once during the 30-year lifetime of their solar panels.

For hybrid installations equipped with battery backup, modern lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) batteries typically feature a 10-year warranty, either defined by chronological time or a guaranteed cycle life (often 4,000 to 6,000 full charge-discharge cycles at 80% Depth of Discharge). Legacy lead-acid solar batteries, by comparison, carry much shorter 3 to 5-year warranties and degrade significantly faster under daily cycling.

The elevated mounting structure that anchors your modules to the roof should carry a 5 to 10-year structural warranty from the installer. In regions subject to heavy monsoon rainfall and high coastal humidity, ensuring your structure uses hot-dip galvanised iron (HDGI) with a minimum coating thickness of 80 microns or anodised aluminium is vital to prevent rust from compromising structural stability.

Common Clauses That Void Solar Warranties in India

Every solar warranty policy includes detailed exclusion clauses. When a component fails, manufacturers inspect the unit closely before approving a replacement. In India, several widespread maintenance and operational errors frequently lead to rejected warranty claims.

First among these is improper cleaning techniques. Cleaning solar modules during the hot afternoon sun causes thermal shock, which can cause micro-cracks in the tempered glass or protective laminate. Furthermore, using abrasive scouring pads, high-pressure industrial washers exceeding manufacturer pressure limits, or acidic cleaning chemicals strips the anti-reflective coating on the glass. Washing should always occur during early morning hours using clean, low-mineral water and soft brushes.

Second, uncertified electrical modifications immediately void both installer and equipment warranties. If a local electrician taps into the dedicated solar DC distribution box (DCDB) to connect additional domestic loads or bypasses the AC surge protection device (SPD) during a routine power trip, any subsequent lightning or surge damage will be rejected by the inverter manufacturer.

Third, physical tampering and missing serial numbers represent an immediate disqualification. Every solar panel carries a barcode and alphanumeric serial number laminated beneath the front glass as well as on the outer frame. If these serial stickers are peeled off, painted over, or damaged, manufacturers will refuse to honour the warranty, as the unit can no longer be matched to its original factory flash test record.

Earthing and surge protection: Inverter manufacturers require certified Class-II surge arrestors and dedicated earthing pits with resistance under 5 ohms. If an inverter suffers a high-voltage surge and inspection reveals missing or unbonded earthing, the warranty claim will be denied as external electrical trauma.

Step-by-Step: How to Claim Under a Solar Warranty

Filing a solar warranty claim in India can be straightforward or painfully slow depending on how well you maintain your documentation. If your system's output drops drastically or an error code appears on your inverter, follow these systematic steps:

  1. Document the fault with photos and logs: Capture clear photographs of the inverter error code display, the affected panel's physical condition, and the barcode sticker on the frame. Download generation logs from your monitoring mobile application showing the drop in output.
  2. Contact your EPC installer: Your installer should visit the site to perform electrical diagnosis, measuring open-circuit voltage (Voc) and short-circuit current (Isc) across individual strings using a calibrated digital multimeter and DC clamp meter.
  3. Initiate the Return Merchandise Authorisation (RMA): If a module or inverter is confirmed defective, the installer submits a formal RMA request to the manufacturer with the original invoice, commissioning sheet, photos, and voltage readings.
  4. Inspection and replacement: For inverters, Tier-1 manufacturers typically dispatch a replacement PCB motherboard or refurbished replacement unit within 7 to 14 business days. For solar panels, the manufacturer may send a field engineer to inspect the module before approving a replacement panel.

Retaining your original commissioning dossier — containing the GST sales invoice, equipment serial number lists, net-meter approval documents from your DISCOM, and the electrical layout single-line diagram (SLD) — is mandatory. Without these papers, tracking warranty entitlements across international or pan-India manufacturers becomes nearly impossible.

How Installer Stability Affects Your 30-Year Protection

A warranty is only as reliable as the company standing behind it. Module and inverter manufacturers often operate large automated factories thousands of kilometres away. When a single residential panel develops a micro-crack or an inverter trips intermittently, the manufacturer will not send a service technician to your rooftop in Srikalahasti or Tirupati. They expect the local EPC contractor to perform the physical troubleshooting, de-installation, warranty submission, and re-installation.

If you purchase your system from an unverified installer who closes down two years later, you are left to manage manufacturer RMA portals, freight shipping, and hazardous high-voltage rooftop electrical work entirely on your own. When evaluating proposals, verify financial longevity, local office presence, and active service teams across your district.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 30-year performance warranty mean my solar panels are fully guaranteed for 30 years?

No. A 30-year performance warranty only guarantees that the photovoltaic cells will produce a specified percentage of their rated power output over time (typically at least 80% to 87.4% after 25 to 30 years). It does not cover physical defects, cracked glass, corroded junction boxes, delamination, or faulty wiring, which fall under the shorter 10 to 15 year product warranty.

Who do I contact if my solar inverter stops working after four years?

You should contact your installer first. While the inverter manufacturer provides the 7 to 10 year equipment warranty, your local installer will diagnose whether the issue is an internal inverter fault, AC/DC grid voltage fluctuation, or external wiring damage, and facilitate the RMA replacement process with the manufacturer.

Will cleaning solar panels with borewell water void my warranty?

Using hard borewell water with high mineral content can create permanent scaling and limescale deposits on the anti-reflective glass coating, which degrades generation and can lead to manufacturer warranty claim rejections if diagnosed as external chemical staining. Always clean panels during early mornings or late evenings using soft or filtered water and soft microfiber brushes.

What happens to my equipment warranties if my original solar installer shuts down?

Your equipment warranties from Tier-1 module, inverter, and battery manufacturers remain legally valid as long as you retain the original purchase invoice, serial numbers, and commissioning documents. However, claiming directly from manufacturers without local installer support involves submitting flash-test data and freight costs, which is why choosing an established company like Ray2Volt Solar is essential.

Is the rooftop mounting structure covered against wind damage and corrosion?

Mounting structure warranties are provided by the installer or structure manufacturer, typically ranging from 5 to 10 years against manufacturing defects and structural corrosion when using hot-dip galvanised iron (HDGI) or anodised aluminium. Damage caused by uncertified modifications, loose anchor bolts, or winds exceeding the structural design rating is excluded.
